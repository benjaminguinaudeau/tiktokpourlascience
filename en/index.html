Did TikTok fuel the violence of autumn 2026 in France?

In autumn 2026, France saw a high-school student movement, with school blockades on 1 October, then demonstrations on 17 October marked by clashes. The authorities then considered temporarily suspending TikTok. We analysed nearly one million public videos.

The study

Nearly one million videos, from 1 September to 29 October

Several hundred high schools blockaded on 1 and 2 October, between 550,000 and 800,000 demonstrators on 17 October. We studied the videos posted from France about these mobilisations. Their audience follows these two waves.

  • 964,000 videos
  • 16 billion views
  • 4.5 million comments
Read the full note (PDF, 30 pages)

Findings

Three accusations against TikTok, tested against public videos

Social media faced three accusations. Here is what we found.

1 Was TikTok used to organise the violence?

Calls to demonstrate circulated widely. Calls for violence, very little.

Locations announced in advance, links to private groups, identical messages: such traces are rare.

Fewer than 0.03% of comments link to a private group.

2 Did images of violence dominate?

Violence accounts for 14% of views, less than demands or humour.

A video can fall into several categories, so the shares do not add up.

Violent images come first and foremost from the media.

"Small creators" have fewer than 50,000 followers; "others" are influencers and anonymous pages.

Under these videos, violence is condemned far more than it is celebrated.

Explicit calls for violence make up 0.27% of all comments.

3 Was the violence artificially amplified?

Showing violence earns small creators no views, but brings the media many.

Content labelled "AI" makes up less than 3% of views. None of our six indicators reveals large-scale foreign interference.

What these results do not say. They cover public videos. They do not rule out mechanisms that escape our observation, in private exchanges or in personalised recommendations. That is why we need your watch history.

Sharing your history

What you share, and how

  1. You request a copy of your data from TikTok.
  2. You upload the ZIP file to the University of Zurich platform. It is read in your browser.
  3. You see exactly what would be transmitted and tick what you agree to give. Nothing is sent before you agree.

What we receive

  • The list of videos shown to you since 1 January 2026 (link and date).

Optionally, and separately: liked videos, favourite videos, username, answers to a few short questions.

What we do not receive

  • Your searches
  • Your messages and comments
  • Your contacts, date of birth or location
  • The content of your videos

These data never leave your device.

Good to know

  • Open to people aged 18 or over. Participation is voluntary; you can stop at any time.
  • Your data are transmitted and stored encrypted on a University of Zurich server in Switzerland. Raw donations are deleted by 31 December 2027.
