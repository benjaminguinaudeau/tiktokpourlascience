The study

Three arguments put to the test

Following the violence of autumn 2026, the French authorities considered temporarily suspending TikTok and other social media platforms. We test the three arguments put forward: coordination, contagion through images and artificial amplification.

1 Coordination

Instructions on where to gather circulate. Calls for violence, very little.

Videos calling for violence gather at most 0.11% of the views of videos about the mobilisations, depending on the day.

2 Contagion

Violence accounts for 14% of views, less than demands or humour.

A video can fall into several categories, so the shares do not add up.

2 Contagion

Violent images come first and foremost from the media.

The media post very little, but account for more than half of the views of violent videos.

2 Reception

Under these videos, violence is condemned far more than it is celebrated.

Explicit calls for violence make up 0.27% of comments.

3 Amplification

Showing violence earns small creators no views, but brings the media many.

No sign of major artificial amplification.

What these results do not say. They do not rule out mechanisms that escape our observation, in particular in private exchanges or personalised recommendations. That is why we need you.

Sharing your history

What you share

How it works

  • Open to people aged 18 or over, on a voluntary basis
  • You request a copy of your data from TikTok
  • You upload the ZIP file, which is read in your browser
  • Nothing is transmitted without your consent

What we receive

  • The list of videos shown to you since 1 January 2026 (link and date)

Optionally, and separately: liked or favourite videos, your username, answers to a few short questions.

What we do not receive

  • Your searches
  • Your messages and comments
  • Your contacts or location
  • The content of your videos
